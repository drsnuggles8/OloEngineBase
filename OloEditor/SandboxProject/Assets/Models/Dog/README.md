# Dog: the furred showcase body

The body the showcase coat is grown on (issue #1533): an original, scruffy golden-retriever-type
mutt, stylised-realistic, with a big head, a short muzzle, big eyes set forward, floppy ears, a deep
chest and a thick tail. It is an original design. No asset, name or likeness of any film character
is used, and every file here is generated by `build_dog.py` in this repository.

The coat is grown here too, in Blender: `build_dog_groom.py` grows it on the exported body and
writes it as Alembic hair curves, which the engine cooks into `Grooms/Dog/Dog.ologroom` (see
[The coat](#the-coat)).

## Files

| file | what it is |
|---|---|
| `build_dog.py` | the Blender 5.x script that writes everything below, from nothing, and runs the next one |
| `build_dog_groom.py` | the coat: grown on `Dog.gltf`, written to `.dog-groom/Dog.abc` at the repository root |
| `Dog.gltf` + `Dog.bin` | one skinned mesh (~256k triangles) on a 50-bone rig, eight materials, six clips |
| `DogCoatColor.png` | 2048² coat colours: DogSkin's albedo and the groom's colour map |
| `DogNoseNormal.png`, `DogNoseOcclusion.png` | 2048² each, the nose leather's own UV square: its cobblestones, and the occlusion that keeps its nostrils dark |
| `DogIrisColor.png` | 1024², the eyeball's albedo on a planar UV across the gaze: the painted iris and the sclera round it |
| `Dog.rig.json` | eye radius, eye centres and gaze, lid and socket radii, the clip list |
| `DogEyeball.gltf` + `.bin` | the unit sphere each eye entity draws (128 x 64: the engine's primitive sphere reloads at 16 segments) |

Units are metres, Y-up, and the head faces +Z, the convention `Horse.gltf` and `Fox.gltf` use. The
paws stand on y = 0. Every node transform is the identity: the engine's importer applies an armature
node's scale twice.

## The contract with the engine

**Bones.** `root`, `pelvis`, `spine_01..03`, `neck_01..02`, `head`, `jaw`, `tongue_01..03`,
`ear_{L,R}_01..03`, `eye_{L,R}`, `lid_{upper,lower}_{L,R}`, `brow_{L,R}`, the forelegs
(`scapula`, `upperarm`, `forearm`, `wrist`, `paw_front`), the hind legs (`thigh`, `shin`, `hock`,
`paw_rear`), each `_L` and `_R`, and `tail_01..06`. `root`, `eye_L` and `eye_R` deform nothing.

**Materials.** `DogSkin` (the pelt), `DogNose`, `DogLip`, `DogGum`, `DogTongue`, `DogTeeth`, `DogPad`
and `DogLid` (the outer skin of the eyelid shells). The scene patches skin profiles over the bare
skins by name through a `MaterialOverridesComponent`, and the coat grows only on `DogSkin` and
`DogLid`. Each nostril's inside, past 1.5 mm into the leather, is `DogPad`: matte, dark, with no wet
film to catch the light, which read as a pair of glass beads.

**Eyes.** Not in the glTF. `SkinOcularSurface` needs each eye to be its own uniformly scaled sphere
whose +Z is the optical axis, so the scene draws two sphere entities attached to `eye_L` and `eye_R`
with a `BoneAttachmentComponent`, placed from `Dog.rig.json`. The eyelids are shells around the eye
centre that rotate about each eye's own lid axis, so a blink can never cut into the eyeball.

**Clips** (30 fps, in place):

| clip | length | plays |
|---|---|---|
| `Idle` | 4 s, loops | breathing, two blinks, an ear twitch, a gentle wag, a small head drift |
| `HeadTilt` | 2.5 s, once | the head rolls about 16°, brows raise, the ears flop with it, back |
| `Sit` | 3 s, holds | the pelvis pitches up and drops onto the rump, the hind legs fold flat |
| `Walk` | 1 s, loops | a four-beat walk, pads planted through the stance |
| `Pant` | 2 s, loops | the mouth open, the tongue slid out over the lip, the chest heaving |
| `Rest` | 1 s, loops | the rest pose held, for look development and evidence frames |

A one-shot clip holds its last frame when played with `loop = false`
(`AnimationStateComponent::m_RequestedLoop`, or `PlayClip(name, false)` from Lua).

## How the body is made

`build_dog.py` sculpts the dog as a signed distance field of rounded primitives, meshes it with
surface nets, repairs the mesh to a manifold, decimates it against a size field (fine on the bare
face, coarse where the long coat covers the torso), refines and relaxes it back onto the field, and
cuts every material boundary into the mesh as an exact contour, so a close-up shows no sawtooth
where nose leather meets fur. The eyelids, tongue and teeth are separate pieces of the same mesh.
Each eye sits in a spherical socket under a thin skin (`f_orbit_skin`) that leaves an opening a
few degrees wider than the lids' almond, so the lids show as a band round the eye and slide under
the skin in a blink; the almond is narrower than the globe and wider at its inner corner, where the
bridge of the nose covers it. The rules and what each one fixed:
[creature-eye-region.md](../../../../../docs/agent-rules/creature-eye-region.md).
Weights are Blender's bone heat, solved on a 100x scaled copy and then constrained region by region
(the jaw's pull fades over the throat instead of stopping at the chin). A bone that moves one part
keeps only the neighbouring skin near that part, fading with the distance from the part's own
surface: bone heat gave the ear roots the crown between the ears, and every ear twitch lifted the
back of the head with the ear. Measured from the bone's head instead, one point, the skin along the
rest of the attachment lost the weight the ear beside it kept, and the twitch tore it open. The UVs are unique and
non-overlapping, because the groom keys its colour map and clump cells on the root UV. The coat
colours are a per-vertex field baked into the texture, with every empty texel filled so mipmaps do
not bleed black across a seam.

## The coat

`build_dog_groom.py` imports `Dog.gltf`, the exact mesh the engine binds to, and grows the coat on
it: regions from the skin's bones and the rig, a flow field smoothed across region seams, layers of
undercoat, guard hair, long hair and strays per region, Voronoi locks, darkened roots and
sun-bleached tips. It writes one hair object per coat group into `.dog-groom/Dog.abc`: ~410k
strands, 57 MB. The archive is a build output, git-ignored, and it stays out of `SandboxProject/Assets`,
because the editor registers everything it finds there.

Blender's Alembic exporter writes a hair object's points and widths and its custom properties, and
nothing else a groom needs. So each group's coat description (`groom_role`, `groom_tint`,
`groom_stiffness`, ...) and each curve's guide flag and root UV ride as custom properties on its
curves datablock. `AlembicGroomImporter` reads them; its header documents the convention.

To comb the coat by hand, run the script's `build()` in a live Blender session, sculpt the
`DogGroom` collection, and call `export_groom_abc(path)`. The per-curve properties are rebuilt from
the curves' `groom_guide` and `surface_uv_coordinate` attributes, which follow the curves through
sculpting, so added and deleted strands export consistently. Then cook it (below).

## Regenerating

From the repository root:

```
blender -b --factory-startup --python OloEditor/SandboxProject/Assets/Models/Dog/build_dog.py -- OloEditor/SandboxProject/Assets/Models/Dog
```

To also render previews of the rest pose, every clip, a blink and the pant, pass a second directory:

```
blender -b --factory-startup --python OloEditor/SandboxProject/Assets/Models/Dog/build_dog.py -- OloEditor/SandboxProject/Assets/Models/Dog build/dog-preview
```

A full build takes about two minutes, the coat included. Look at the previews before committing: a
pose can read correctly at rest and fold a leg through the ground in motion. The coat alone takes
about fifteen seconds:

```
blender -b --factory-startup --python OloEditor/SandboxProject/Assets/Models/Dog/build_dog_groom.py
```

Then cook it into the scene, from `OloEditor/`:

```
OLO_DOG_EXPORT=1 OloEngine-Tests --gtest_filter=DogShowcaseEvidenceTest.ExportsTheLiveScene
```

`OLO_DOG_ABC=1` makes the evidence draw straight from the `.abc` instead of the shipped groom, for
look development. The coat's own tuning knobs are environment variables read by
`build_dog_groom.py`: `OLO_DOG_FINE`, `OLO_DOG_TAIL`, `OLO_DOG_DENSITY`, `OLO_DOG_FLOW_PASSES` and
`OLO_DOG_REGION_DEBUG` (each described where it is read).
