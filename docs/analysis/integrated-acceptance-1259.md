# Integrated AAA acceptance (#1259)

The acceptance gate of the #1225 programme: one generated scene that holds every subject at once,
captured on every backend and path, measured under one protocol. This file is the contract (scene,
cameras, manifests, protocol, gate) and, below it, the results. Decisions taken with the owner on
2026-10-10 are recorded on the issue
([comment](https://github.com/drsnuggles8/OloEngineBase/issues/1259#issuecomment-6094959081)).
The October results below are the first baseline; the
[post-stabilization baseline of 2026-11-28](#post-stabilization-release-baseline-2026-11-28-1799)
(#1799) follows them at the end of this page.

## Gate

**p95 frame time at or under 16.667 ms with virtual shadow maps on**, native 1920x1080, every
backend x path cell, on the RTX 4090 named below, Release with symbols. A cell passes only if every
counted run passes. Skin, fur and flora are never disabled or thinned to meet it; a miss stays an
open item with an issue.

## Scene: `Scenes/Benchmark/Acceptance.olo`

Generated by `scripts/perf/generate-acceptance-scene.py` from committed scenes (never hand-edited):

| Subject | Source | What it is in this scene |
|---|---|---|
| Flora traversal | `FoliageMeadowToWoodland.olo` | 256 m terrain, six foliage layers (meadow grass, wildflowers, shrubs, leaf litter, dune grass, woodland pines with impostors), sky, shadowed sun; scene wind on (Meadow's wind block) |
| Moving population | `Benchmark/AnimalPopulation.olo` | 41 foxes (hero, 4 featured, 36 herd) with bound coats, turned onto the traversal heading so the herd stands 8-78 m along the meadow-to-woodland walk; every animal walks a closed-form path and follows the ground |
| Short and long coat | `GroomAnimals.olo` | the bound short-coat and long-coat horses, walking in the meadow beside the traversal start, coats parented to their bodies |
| Digital-human close-up | `GroomAnimals.olo`, `DigitalHuman.olo` | the rigged InfiniteScanHead (LookAround clip, skin profile and measured thickness) with its bound hair, on a plinth in a studio beside the terrain, lit by DigitalHuman's key and side lights with the sun as backlight. Separable eyes and mouth do not exist (#1402) |
| Moving light | `Benchmark/Courtyard.olo` | a shadowed point light ("Moving Lantern") carried over the horse station by `EntityMotion` |
| Interaction | `FoliageInteractionComponent` | on the 16 subjects nearest the cameras (the field has 16 slots) |

The traversal's flora was authored for #1224's test walk, not for a picture: 30 m of relief left most
of the ground steep enough to be rock (which excludes every plant), the tops were snow, the woodland
had 19 pines, and 1.2-2.6 m grass hid a fox entirely. The acceptance scene keeps every layer, mesh
and density but sets a 16 m relief, drops the snow rule, gives the grasses meadow heights
(0.45-1.15 m) and doubles the pine density with a wider moisture band, so the walk runs from open
meadow into a wood. These are content choices for legibility, stated here because they change the
workload; nothing was thinned.

Cameras and the moving light are placed from `scripts/perf/acceptance-ground.json`, an 8 m grid of
the real terrain height exported by `AcceptanceSceneTest.ExportsTheGroundHeightGrid`
(`OLO_ACCEPTANCE_GROUND_EXPORT=1`), because the generator cannot evaluate the procedural terrain.
`AcceptanceSceneTest.EveryManifestCameraClearsTheTerrainAlongItsPath` checks every manifest camera
against the height field itself along its whole trajectory (lowest clearance 2.8 m).

Two engine gaps closed for it: animals now stand on the terrain (`AnimalPathComponent::FollowTerrain`),
and a benchmark capture now walks the animal paths (an edit-mode tick never ran the "AnimalPaths"
system, so every earlier capture showed the herd walking on the spot).

## Measurement protocol

`scripts/perf/acceptance-benchmark.py` enforces it:

* Release with symbols only (`/Z7` in the `dev-cached` tree), with a declared compiler. Never compare across builds.
* Interleaved, repeated, fresh-process runs: cells round-robin with the start cell rotated each round, at least three counted runs per cell.
* Other GPU load is checked before every run and sampled every 30 s during it. A run that overlaps
  another GPU process or a build is kept on disk but not counted, and the process is named.
* Per run, per camera: actual internal and display sizes, the resolved upscaler, VSM selected;
  p50/p95/p99/max and deadline misses for frame time, CPU, fence wait, present wait, recording wall
  and join wait, total GPU (valid, distinct frame ids only), and per-pass GPU distributions.
* Provenance per binary: binary, shader-tree and asset hashes, commit, dirty diff and untracked files.
* Statistics are reported as a range across runs; frames are never resampled. The GL test host times
  `Scene::OnUpdateEditor` and the editor host times the completed-frame interval; they are never pooled.

## Results

**The gate is not met on any cell.** Every camera of every counted run misses 16.667 ms p95, on both
backends and all three raster paths, and in the hybrid cell on Vulkan. Nothing was disabled or
thinned to get closer. Each miss below is attributed to the pass or CPU scope that causes it, and each
cause has an open issue (see *Open work*).

Hardware: NVIDIA GeForce RTX 4090 (driver 617.14), Intel Core i7-14700KF, Windows 11 (10.0.22631),
64 GB. Build: Release with symbols, clang-cl, `dev-cached` tree. Native 1920x1080, VSM on, TAA on,
MSAA off. The GL protocol checked for other GPU load before and during every run and found none; the
live Vulkan sessions ran outside that check.

**Hybrid never traces at these cameras.** The hybrid rows come from a second editor session, after
the generator was fixed to make every shadowed light request the ray-traced tier (the first
session's hybrid cell traced no light at all and measured as Deferred). With the tier armed, the
TLAS holds 111–369 instances and all 44 coats are represented, but the vegetation planner leaves
94–144 of its 148–431 casting groups out over the per-frame build budget, so the shadow TLAS is
withheld (`pressure: frameWork`) at every camera and the lights take the counted raster fallback.
That is #1578, which area-sized card groups narrowed but did not close. The cell still pays
26–75 ms of CPU in `Vegetation::Queue` for proxies no ray reads.

Arming the lights exposed a bug, fixed on this branch: the vegetation and groom producers ran on
every Deferred frame whose lights asked for the tier, even with raster shadows. That cost the Deferred
cell 24–39 ms (frame p50 72 → 30 ms at head-backlit, 190 → 136 ms at population-dolly after the fix).
The Forward, Forward+ and Deferred rows above come from the first session, which had neither the
light requests nor the waste, and agree with the post-fix Deferred check within 10%.

GPU memory at the end of the session: live 2.75 GiB, peak 6.26 GiB; ShadowPass 696 MiB, ShadowMap
640 MiB, unattributed 427 MiB, transient pool 273 MiB, GroomPass 199 MiB. In this session (the
#1259 build, 2026-10-11) the editor process held 6.6–6.8 GB of private memory throughout, and a
reload of the scene peaked at 12.9 GB for about 30 s. The #1591 measurements are a separate pair of
Release builds on the same machine, reloading the scene in a Vulkan editor and sampling private bytes
every 0.5 s: master `4c7832d2a` peaked at 15.4 GB and settled at 6.4 GB; the #1591 branch peaked at
6.3 GB and settled at 6.2 GB, with the strand cache inside its budget throughout the load. The editor log has no
shader error; it warns that the groom strand cache runs 50–85 MiB

### Where the time goes

**The frame is CPU-bound on both backends.** CPU p50 is within 2–4 ms of frame p50 on every row
above. An attribution pass on the same Vulkan editor (Deferred, `--scope-samples 8 --groom-stats`,
CPU scopes averaged over 8 frames; scopes nest, so columns do not add):

| camera | frame p50 | `Scene::OnUpdateEditor` | `Groom::Deform` | `UploadDeformation` | `Simulate` | `RootEvaluate` | `PackFrame` | `RG::Execute` | coats strand / card |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| head-closeup | 196 | 218 | 94 | 70 | 49 | 44 | 34 | 115 | 1 / 43 |
| head-backlit | 30 | 50 | 7 | 9 | 3 | 3 | 3 | 40 | 1 / 2 |
| coats-closeup | 96 | 177 | 59 | 64 | 27 | 31 | 34 | 111 | 2 / 16 |
| population-dolly | 137 | 268 | 128 | 119 | 63 | 63 | 60 | 131 | 2 / 36 |
| rapid-turn | 107 | 109 | 59 | 38 | 31 | 28 | 20 | 44 | 0 / 28 |

1. **Bound-coat deformation on the main thread** (59–128 ms wherever the herd is in view). Every
   published coat runs guide simulation, root evaluation, packing and the deformation upload,
   whatever tier it is drawn at, and 16–43 of the coats in view are on the card tier. The view
   cull this branch adds removes coats outside the frustum and its shadow sweep; it cannot help the
   ones in view. A card-tier simulation skip was tried here and reverted (it broke seven groom
   tests); #1588.
2. **Render-graph recording** (`RG::Execute`, 40–131 ms of CPU). Per-pass CPU puts `GroomPass` at
   34–76 ms of it and `ShadowPass` at 5–11 ms, every other pass under 1 ms: the strand pass's CPU
   side, which acquires each coat's deformed geometry before recording, is the second groom cost on
   the main thread (#1588).
3. **GroomPass on OpenGL** at the head close-up: 88–120 ms of GPU, of which `GroomPass/RootFrames`
   is 36–75 ms and about 45 ms falls outside every GroomPass sub-bracket. Single-frame snapshots of
   the same framing on Vulkan read 5–26 ms (`RootFrames` 7 ms), so the GL deformation upload path
   is the suspect (#1589).
4. **ShadowPass** with VSM: 35–59 ms on GL and 24–31 ms on Vulkan at the outdoor cameras. Its only
   foliage brackets read under 0.2 ms, so the cost is unattributed beyond them. A CSM control run
   (`acceptance-forward-csm`, one GL run, ShadowPass p50) splits it:

   | camera | VSM (gate) | CSM | `ShadowPass/FoliageCasters` under CSM |
   |---|---:|---:|---:|
   | head-closeup | 18–25 | 940 | 66 |
   | head-backlit | 9–10 | 538 | 33 |
   | coats-closeup | 35–45 | 149 | 2 |
   | population-dolly | 47–59 | 25 | 3 |
   | rapid-turn | 42–45 | 149 | 3 |

   Where no shadowed lamp is near, the VSM costs the sun 25–35 ms more than CSM. Where one is (the
   head's key and side lights, the lantern over the horses), CSM sends the lamps through the shadow
   atlas, and that route costs 0.1–0.9 s of GPU, almost none of it in the foliage bracket. Which
   casters it spends that on is not bracketed (#1590).

   The table predates two changes to what those lamps draw. The VSM's local-light layers draw
   groom casters since #1592, and since #1634 the head's hair and both horse coats carry a
   `GroomSceneShadowComponent`, so they cast and receive scene shadows; before, only the fox coats
   did. The cost of #1634, measured A/B on GL Deferred (Release, 1920×1080, median of 100 frames,
   2026-10-25, with other GPU work on the machine):

   | camera | VSM: ShadowPass | of it, VSM local-layer groom casters | VSM off (atlas): ShadowPass | draw calls (VSM) | groom local-layer draws (VSM) | groom atlas draws (VSM off) |
   |---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
   | head-closeup | +40 ms | +30 ms | +39 ms | +194 | +18 (3 lamps × 6) | +24 to +48 |
   | coats-closeup | +16 ms | +3.5 ms | +34 ms | +254 | +6 (1 lamp × 6) | +12 |

   MSAA 4 on Deferred gives the same picture (+42 / +23 ms ShadowPass). The groom draw counts are
   `olo_groom_budget_stats` in a live Vulkan editor; with VSM off the head's atlas count steps between
   frames (#1646).
5. **Frame-time tails on Vulkan**: the p95/p99 spread at the dolly and rapid-turn cameras is CPU
   hitches of 0.65–2.1 s (GPU time unchanged in those frames), which grew over the session. They did
   not recur in 1,082 frames polled one CPU-scope snapshot per frame at the same poses, so their
   cause is not attributed here (#1594).

### What the captures show

Every gate camera on every GL raster path is captured as `Acceptance_GL_<Path>_<camera>.png` in the
git-ignored `OloEditor/assets/tests/visual/`, written by `AcceptanceSceneTest.EveryGateCameraSeesItsSubjectOnEveryRasterPath`.
Motion footage and AOVs come from the GL test host (`acceptance-*-motion`, one frame every second
frame for 2 s per camera, at the protocol's fixed 1/60 s step with the animal paths walking). They
were written to [evidence/integrated-acceptance-1259/](../testing/evidence/integrated-acceptance-1259/)
and are no longer tracked there (see *Raw run output* below):

* `GL_Deferred_<camera>.webp`: the Beauty sequence for each of the five cameras, animated.
* `GL_<Path>_<camera>_aov.jpg`: six evenly spaced frames per row. Deferred has Beauty, Albedo,
  Normals and Velocity; Forward and Forward+ have Beauty and Velocity. Velocity PNGs are min–max
  normalised per frame over all channels, so their tones compare within a frame only.
* `GL_Deferred_lighting_taps.jpg`: the five lighting terms (#1526) at the head and coat close-ups.
  These AOVs found a bug, fixed on this branch: the strand shader never read the tap, so every coat
  carried its full colour into all five terms. It now splits the cuticle reflection (specular) from
  the light that entered the fibre (diffuse), and lights from the environment, and
  `LightingTapEvidenceTest` holds a lit tuft to the partition.

The footage shows the herd walking through the grass on the terrain, the horses walking under the
moving lantern, and the head turning with its hair following the body it is bound to.

Known shortfalls in the picture, all open:

* **No separable eyes or mouth** on the scan head (#1402), so criterion 1's eye and mouth part is unmet.
* **The head's hair** reads as a thin, smeared veil over the forehead and eyes (#1593): the crown is
  sparse and the strands hang straight over the cheek and past the shoulder line without resolving
  into clumps. The lighting taps show why it reads grey: the pigment is dark brown, and the cuticle
  sheen (the R lobe, from the environment above all) is a broad grey on top of it. Vulkan shows the
  same (`VK_Hybrid_head-*.jpg`).
* **Herd coats do not cast into the scene shadow maps** (the hero and four featured foxes do): an
  authoring choice from the fox-coat work, because each casting coat holds caster entries per shadow
  step. Herd bodies still cast.
* Deferred cannot take a dynamic internal scale (#1537), and skinned virtual geometry has no RT proxy
  (#1525); those cells are recorded as unreachable rather than measured.

No longer a shortfall: the lamps now shadow the head's hair and the horse coats (#1592's routing,
#1634's components). The hair shadows the neck under the key and side lights: with VSM on, 3.0% of
the GL Deferred head-closeup frame darkens against the scene without the components
(`Acceptance_GL_Deferred_head-closeup.png`). At the lantern the horses' skinned bodies already
cast, so the coats change only 0.15% of that frame.

Not measured in this pass: the `acceptance-*-upscale-quality` manifests and the Forward+ and Deferred
`-csm` manifests. They are generated and pass the terrain-clearance check; one Forward CSM run is the
control above. With every camera missing the gate by 3x or more at native resolution, an upscaled cell
would not change the verdict, and the CPU-bound frame would not get faster at a lower internal scale.

### Editor capture stalls

Two of 14 Vulkan editor attempts stopped completing frames mid-capture for 10–14 minutes and were
not counted: `vk-hybrid` round 1 (head-backlit, frame 386, silent from 11:45:03 for 844 s, just after
a same-size window resize) and `vk-deferred` round 2 (coats-closeup, frame 250, silent from 12:24:14
for 607 s, just after a horse coat built its stride-1 REST geometry). The editor recovered both times
and ran the capture's cleanup. Neither preceding event explains it (the stride-1 build occurs in 28
runs that did not stall), counted runs never go silent for more than 102 s, and the GL host never
stalled. The reruns counted. Filed with the logs as #1871. The failed records in the evidence say
`warmupTimedOut: true`: the editor sets that flag for a frame-await timeout in the measured loop as
well, so it does not place the stall in the warm-up.
